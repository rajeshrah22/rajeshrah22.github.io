There exists no scripture out there that says God will be pleased with the
homelabbers; moreover, my future wife most definitely won't be impressed
by my Linux terminal skills. So, why maintain a homelab then?

Is it fun? Sometimes. Depends on who you are. What if you're a busy person
with little time for side ventures? Wow you're spending your vacation time
from your main job working on Linux to work on Linux at home `:)`. Can't
escape the computer I guess.

I will give you an intro into why I am doing it and what my goals are. It will
serve as a written record of what my destination is. Where is the north star?

## For the love of the game

I mean, why did I enter the world of software engineering in the first place?
I had an inclination towards solving problems in Middle and High school: doing
well in maths and my Computer Science courses. Later, I decided to get into
IT / Cloud infra and systems programming in college. This was the stuff I was
genuinely interested in. How does an OS function? What does my program do at the
hardware level? Maintaining a homelab is very much in this realm of developing
and understanding computer systems.

In my job, I have the narrow task of reproducing and fixing specifig bugs related
to nmstate and NetworkManager, which are network configuration tools for linux.
This usually doesn't incentivise nderstanding the entirety of the system.
I don't need to worry about the firewalld config of the end user in most
cases, how to exactly reproduce their OCP cluster, or even their entire network
config. So, there is some room for learning here in terms of operations and
system configuration.

## Operations, DevOps, and Site Reliablity

You can start learning the principles and techniques of maintaing infrastructure
at a scale and pace that you can digest. I bet some of the lessons learned on
a single computer can be scaled to many computers in the future.

Things like how to manage software than needs regular updates, managing configuration
of the software, backing up data for worst case scenarios, I wouldn't learn from
just fixing arbitrary bugs on a single network configuration tool.

## For the Flex

I mean, I think other tech nerds will appreciate a homelab. Can be a good way to connect
with them in interviews, conferences, networking events, etc.
